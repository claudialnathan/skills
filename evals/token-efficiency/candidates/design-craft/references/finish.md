# Static finish and verification

Use this symptom lookup for the accepted visual concern. Treat every pattern as
a diagnostic relationship with a product/brief/local-grammar boundary, not as
a universal recipe.

## Shape and radius

Inspect nested radii when two rounded surfaces are meant to read as one
container relationship. Concentric curves often result when the inner radius
accounts for the intervening padding, but intentionally independent shapes do
not need to be concentric. Reuse the product’s radius roles before adding a
local value; promote only a shared semantic role.

Verify at the rendered size. A formula cannot prove optical continuity.

## Separation and static depth

Choose border, shadow, background shift, backdrop treatment, or no additional
separation from:

- whether two surfaces must be distinguished;
- whether one surface is meant to feel elevated;
- the actual background in light, dark, and high-contrast states;
- the product’s established depth grammar.

A flat hairline can be correct. A shadow can be correct. Layered effects are
not automatically more polished. Keep temporal choreography and animated
shadow/blur only when their purpose, interruption, performance, and reduced
alternative are separately proven.

## Typography and numbers

Use type size, weight, contrast, measure, wrapping, and spacing to express
semantic hierarchy. Check x-height and rendered line boxes before “fixing” a
font-size number.

Use tabular numerals where alignment or changing digits would otherwise shift:
counters, timers, aligned prices, tables, and metrics. Do not apply them to all
static prose merely because the cost seems small. Apply balanced or improved
wrapping only to roles and content where it survives narrow widths,
localization, and the supported browser floor.

Use platform font smoothing only when rendered comparison shows an issue, such
as transformed text on a relevant macOS target. Do not install it as global
polish by reflex.

## Color and accents

Start from semantic roles. Use the smallest contrast or color change that
distinguishes hierarchy, state, and affordance. One accent per view is a useful
quiet default, not a law: a brief or established product grammar may support
several accents when each retains a clear role.

Check light, dark, forced/high-contrast, disabled, invalid, selected, and focus
states as applicable. Color alone must not carry state or action meaning.

## Images and icons

Choose image edge treatment from the source image, surrounding background, and
product grammar. A subtle neutral outline can stabilize an unframed image
against a similar background; a tinted, photographic, already-framed, or
deliberately borderless image may need another treatment or none. Avoid one
pure-black/pure-white recipe across every context.

Keep icon family, stroke/fill, optical size, and visual weight coherent with
the surrounding type. Use filled/outlined differences when they signal state,
not as arbitrary mixture. Preserve recognizable platform and brand marks.

## Affordance and product character

Use shape, contrast, labeling, icon treatment, and static state cues to make
controls recognizable. A visual treatment does not replace semantics, focus,
keyboard behavior, touch geometry, announcements, recovery, or interaction
testing.

Generic gradients, glow, dramatic card depth, oversized headlines, and
decorative icon grids deserve inspection when they substitute for product
meaning. Keep them when the brief or local grammar establishes their character
and the result remains legible and proportionate.

## Verification

For each changed treatment:

1. render realistic content in the primary state;
2. render one applicable pressure state—long/localized content, empty,
   disabled, invalid, focus, dark, or high contrast;
3. compare the intended perceptual order with the selected local exemplar;
4. inspect computed style when token, inheritance, color, font, border, or
   shadow propagation is part of the claim;
5. measure contrast and accessible state distinction with the project’s
   configured tools where applicable;
6. capture a screenshot or concrete rendered observation that shows the
   claimed effect;
7. run the focused static checks owned by the project.

Record the state, mechanism, artifact, and result. Source inspection proves
ownership; it does not prove appearance. A screenshot proves one rendered
state; it does not prove semantics or all themes. Mark every unrun claim
`Unverified`.
